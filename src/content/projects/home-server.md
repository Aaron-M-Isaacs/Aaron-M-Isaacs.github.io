## Context
In college, while I was getting my degree in Electrical Engineering, my roommate had started getting more and more into computers and running servers. I had found this to be extremely fascinating, after all, both of us had started building computers from a very young age, and this was a side of computing that was completely foreign to me. After a while, I had encountered a 'ship of Theseus' moment with my computer, and realized that I could Frankenstein a new (old) computer out my old PC parts. Not entirely sure what I should do with a spare computer, I asked my friend if he would teach me more about starting my own home server.

## Problem
I knew nothing about Linux, let alone servers, and how they ran, what they were, networking, nothing. This was my first time experiencing something along the lines of working with servers, bash, linux, anything that had to do with running a computer on something other than windows. But I wanted to learn, and I had a relatively simple goal in mind: I want to replace my use of Google Drive, with something that I can have on my own server. 

## Approach
At first I had help from my roommate, he taught me how to set up Ubuntu Linux, he showed me initially on how to use bash commands, where to find more of them and understand them better, he taught me how to generate key-pairs so that I could set up SSH and remote into my server from my laptop, and generally speaking he got me pointed in the right direction for most things, including NextCloud, a service that more or less acts like a home-storage solution of Google Drive. He also helped me figure out how to buy and use my own domain name so that I can eventually access my NextCloud instance from the internet anywhere, not just from our home network.

## Outcome
At the time, NextCloud was not easy to set up. As someone who was starting from scratch, relatively brand new with help from a friend, it still took me months before I was successful. But in that time I learned more about servers, computers, networking, and a whole lot more that I never would have in my time at college. Especially since I was getting a degree in Electrical Engineering with a minor in computer science.

Today, that server is still running, and I've had the pleasure of being able to set up a few more services on it that I wouldn't have been able to do had I not done that beginning project, including a minecraft server that my friends and I can enjoy together. Learning to set up this server and learn the ways of being a server admin has perhaps been one of my proudest accomplishments, and I'm glad that I can still reap the benefits of those learnings even today.
